Late-Term Abortion in Uganda: Information & Support

📅 Updated: September 10, 2026 ⏱ 7 min read
👨‍⚕️ Reviewed by Our Medical Team

Late-term abortion — sometimes called second-trimester abortion — is a medically necessary procedure for women whose pregnancies cannot safely continue. It is one of the most sensitive areas in women's healthcare, and one of the most misunderstood.

This guide explains when late-term abortion is needed, what the procedure involves at Frontlane Medical Center, and how to access confidential support in Uganda. It's written for women and families facing this difficult decision — with honesty, medical accuracy, and compassion.

Medical Disclaimer: Late-term abortion in Uganda is only legally permitted in specific circumstances defined by the Penal Code Act and Ministry of Health guidelines. At Frontlane Medical Center, we provide these procedures only where the law allows. If you are unsure whether your situation qualifies, contact us confidentially for guidance.

What Is Late-Term Abortion?

Late-term abortion refers to pregnancy termination between 13 and 25 weeks of gestation. It is performed after the first trimester, when the pregnancy is more advanced.

In Uganda, late-term abortion is legally restricted to specific circumstances. It is not available on request. Every case is assessed individually by a specialist doctor to confirm that it qualifies under Uganda's legal framework.

When Is Late-Term Abortion Medically Necessary?

Late-term abortion is typically considered in the following situations:

1. Severe fetal anomaly incompatible with life

When a scan reveals a severe fetal malformation that means the baby cannot survive outside the womb — or would only survive with severe suffering — many families choose to end the pregnancy. This is one of the most common reasons for late-term procedures.

2. Risk to the mother's life

If continuing the pregnancy poses a direct threat to the mother's life — for example, due to severe cardiac, renal, or hypertensive conditions — termination may be medically necessary.

3. Risk to the mother's physical health

Conditions that would cause serious, lasting damage to the mother's physical health if the pregnancy continued.

4. Risk to the mother's mental health

In cases where continuing the pregnancy would cause severe psychological harm — for example, where the pregnancy resulted from assault — termination may be medically indicated.

5. Rape, incest, or defilement discovered late

Where the pregnancy resulted from rape, incest, or defilement and was not identified until after the first trimester.

Every case is different. A specialist doctor will review your medical history, scan results, and circumstances to determine whether your situation qualifies under Uganda's legal framework.

What Makes the Procedure Different from Early Abortion?

Late-term procedures are more complex than early abortion for several reasons:

Extended pre-procedure assessment

Before the procedure, you'll have a more thorough medical review than for an early abortion. This includes detailed ultrasound, blood work, medical history assessment, and confirmation of legal eligibility.

Performed personally by a specialist doctor

At Frontlane Medical Center, late-term procedures are performed personally by a Senior Gynecologist — not delegated to junior staff. This ensures the highest level of clinical skill for a more complex procedure.

Everything else is the same

Despite the added complexity, patients receiving late-term care at Frontlane receive the same standard of confidentiality, compassion, and aftercare as all our other patients. There is no different treatment based on gestational age — only different medical protocols.

What to Expect — Step by Step

Step 1: Confidential consultation and assessment

You'll meet with a specialist doctor for a confidential consultation. This includes:

  • Detailed discussion of your situation
  • Ultrasound scan to confirm gestational age and identify any fetal anomalies
  • Blood tests and medical history review
  • Confirmation of legal eligibility under Uganda's framework

Step 2: Preparation

If your case qualifies, the doctor will explain the procedure, discuss what to expect, and answer any questions. A signed consent form is required before proceeding.

Step 3: The procedure

Late-term abortion is performed in a sterile environment with appropriate anesthesia and pain management. The procedure is completed under full medical supervision, with your comfort and safety as priority.

Step 4: Recovery and monitoring

After the procedure, you'll be monitored during initial recovery. Most patients are able to return home the same day, with light bleeding or spotting expected for a few days.

Step 5: Follow-up visit

We schedule a follow-up at 14 days (2 weeks) to confirm complete recovery, check for any complications, and discuss family planning.

Recovery After a Late-Term Procedure

Recovery after a late-term abortion is similar to surgical abortion recovery:

  • Feeling back to normal: Usually about 2 days
  • Bleeding: Light bleeding or spotting for a few days
  • Return to work: Usually within 2 days
  • Physical restrictions: Avoid strenuous activity, heavy lifting, and sexual activity until bleeding has stopped

You'll receive medication to manage pain, control bleeding, prevent infection, and support cleansing. The doctor will explain exactly what you're taking and how to use it.

Legal Requirements in Uganda

Late-term abortion in Uganda is only legally permitted in specific circumstances. Depending on your situation, you may be asked to provide:

  • Medical evidence — for fetal anomaly, life risk, or health risk cases
  • Police report — for cases of rape, incest, or defilement
  • Specialist assessment — from your attending doctor confirming eligibility
  • Signed consent — standard for all procedures

Our team will explain exactly what documentation your specific situation requires during a confidential consultation.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is late-term abortion legal in Uganda?

Only in specific circumstances defined by law. It is permitted to preserve the mother's life or physical health, or in cases of severe fetal anomaly, rape, or incest. At Frontlane Medical Center, we provide these procedures only where the law allows.

How much does a late-term abortion cost?

At Frontlane, late-term abortion costs between 400,000 and 800,000 UGX, depending on the exact gestational age and medical complexity. The final price is confirmed during your confidential consultation.

How long does the procedure take?

Late-term procedures take longer than early abortion. Most patients are with us for several hours on the day of the procedure. Full recovery is usually about 2 days.

Will I be judged?

No. Our team provides compassionate, non-judgmental care to every patient. We focus on your health, safety, and privacy — not on judgment.

What support is available for me emotionally?

All patients receive the same compassionate, confidential care regardless of gestational age. This includes on-site counselling, a written emotional recovery guide, and 24/7 access to our support line for any questions or concerns.

What if my situation does not qualify for a legal procedure?

We will tell you honestly and clearly. We'll explain what other options are available — including post-abortion care, family planning counselling, and support services.
