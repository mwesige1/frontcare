Family Planning & Contraception Options After Abortion

📅 Updated: September 10, 2026 ⏱ 7 min read
👨‍⚕️ Reviewed by Our Medical Team

After an abortion, your fertility can return within weeks — often before your first period. If you don't want to become pregnant again, starting contraception early is important. This guide explains all the contraception options available in Uganda, how they work, and how to choose what's right for you.

It's written for women who have recently had an abortion at Frontlane Medical Center, and for anyone seeking confidential family planning advice in Kampala.

Medical Disclaimer: This article is for educational purposes. Contraception choice depends on your health, medical history, and personal circumstances. Always consult a qualified medical practitioner before starting any method.

Why Family Planning Matters After Abortion

Many women are surprised to learn that fertility returns quickly after an abortion — in most cases within 2 to 4 weeks. You can become pregnant again before your next period, even before your body has fully returned to its normal cycle.

That's why we recommend starting contraception right away if you don't want another pregnancy. At Frontlane Medical Center, we discuss family planning at your follow-up visit — and most methods can be started the same day.

Contraception Options Available in Uganda

At Frontlane Medical Center, we offer the full range of modern contraception methods. Our doctors help you choose based on your health, lifestyle, and how long you want protection.

1. Oral Contraceptive Pills

The most widely used method — a daily pill that prevents pregnancy by regulating hormones.

  • How it works: Taken at the same time every day
  • Effectiveness: Over 99% when taken correctly; about 91% with typical use
  • Duration: As long as you continue taking it
  • Benefits: Easy to start and stop; may reduce menstrual cramps and regulate periods
  • Considerations: Requires daily discipline; not suitable for some medical conditions

2. Injectable Contraception

A hormonal injection given every 2 to 3 months — no daily action required.

  • How it works: A single injection every 12–13 weeks
  • Effectiveness: Over 99% with correct use
  • Duration: 2–3 months per injection
  • Benefits: Private, convenient, no daily routine
  • Considerations: May cause irregular bleeding initially; return to fertility can take longer after stopping

3. Contraceptive Implants

A small, flexible rod inserted under the skin of the upper arm — provides years of protection.

  • How it works: Slowly releases hormone into the body
  • Effectiveness: Over 99% — one of the most effective methods available
  • Duration: 3–5 years depending on type
  • Benefits: Nothing to remember; fully reversible; discreet
  • Considerations: Requires a small procedure to insert and remove; may cause irregular bleeding initially

4. Intrauterine Device (IUD / Copper T)

A small device placed inside the uterus — provides long-term protection without hormones (copper) or with low-dose hormone.

  • How it works: Inserted by a doctor into the uterus
  • Effectiveness: Over 99% — among the most effective methods available
  • Duration: 5–10 years depending on type
  • Benefits: Long-lasting, nothing to remember, can be removed any time
  • Considerations: Requires insertion and removal by a trained doctor; may cause heavier periods (copper) or lighter ones (hormonal)

5. Condoms (Male and Female)

The only method that also protects against sexually transmitted infections (STIs).

  • How it works: Physical barrier preventing sperm from reaching the egg
  • Effectiveness: About 98% with perfect use; about 87% with typical use
  • Duration: Single use
  • Benefits: Only method that also prevents STIs, including HIV
  • Considerations: Must be used correctly every time

6. Emergency Contraception (Morning-After Pill)

Emergency contraception is a backup method — used after unprotected sex or when other contraception fails.

  • How it works: Prevents or delays ovulation
  • Effectiveness: Most effective when taken as soon as possible — ideally within 72 hours; some options work up to 120 hours
  • Duration: One-time use per episode
  • Benefits: Available on a walk-in basis at Frontlane Medical Center — no appointment needed
  • Considerations: Not a regular contraception method — only for emergencies

Choosing the Right Method for You

There is no single "best" contraception method — only the best method for you. Our doctors help you choose based on:

  • How long you want protection — from months to years
  • Your health history — some methods are safer for certain conditions
  • Your lifestyle — daily routine, privacy preferences, partner situation
  • STI protection needs — only condoms provide this
  • Side effects you're willing to accept — each method affects women differently
  • Whether you want to become pregnant later — all methods are reversible

During your consultation, we'll discuss your options honestly and help you make an informed decision — without pressure or judgment.

At your follow-up visit at 14 days, we'll discuss family planning and can start most methods the same day. Some methods — like IUDs and implants — are best placed after your uterus has returned to normal, which usually happens within a few weeks.

If you want to start contraception sooner, call us to discuss the timing for your specific situation.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I get pregnant before my first period after an abortion?

Yes. Fertility can return within 2–4 weeks after an abortion — often before your first period. Use contraception if you don't want to become pregnant again.

Which method is best after an abortion?

There's no single best method. Long-acting options like implants and IUDs provide years of protection with no daily action. Injectables last 2–3 months. Pills require daily use. Condoms also protect against STIs. Our doctors help you choose based on your circumstances.

Can I get contraception the same day as my abortion?

Some methods, including pills, injectables, and condoms, can be started immediately. IUDs and implants are usually placed at your follow-up visit when the uterus has returned to normal.

Does contraception affect my fertility long-term?

No. All modern contraception methods are fully reversible. Fertility returns to normal after stopping any method, though it may take a few weeks to a few months for hormonal methods to clear your system.

Do I need my partner's permission?

No. Family planning decisions are entirely yours. We provide confidential services without requiring partner consent at any age.

Is emergency contraception the same as abortion?

No. Emergency contraception prevents pregnancy before it starts by delaying ovulation. It does not terminate an existing pregnancy. If you are already pregnant, emergency contraception will not work.
